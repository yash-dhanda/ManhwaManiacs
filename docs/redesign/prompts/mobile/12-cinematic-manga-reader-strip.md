# Mobile Cinematic manga reader 1: strip, chrome, ruler, credits

Track: mobile · Order 42 · Depends on: `docs/redesign/prompts/mobile/11-cinematic-feature-and-book-pages.md` · Web twin: `docs/redesign/prompts/web/12-cinematic-manga-reader-strip.md` · Proof folder: `docs/redesign/proof/mobile-12/`

## Goal

Build the first half of the Cinematic manga reader in the Flutter app (iOS and Android), as chrome on the shared reader engine that `mobile/00` extracted into `mobile/lib/features/reader/engine/` (`ReaderEngine`, its Riverpod provider, `ReaderEngineState` and the `chromeBuilder(context, ReaderEngineState)` slot). You deliver: the one reader `SwipeablePage` that paints the Column wipe, the Dip or the iOS finger-tracked slide from the entry kind carried in `GoRouterState.extra`; the reader's system-bar choreography of cinematic §8.0.5 (status bar hidden on entry, shown and hidden in step with the chrome, edge-to-edge on exit); the running head and folio bar over their scrims; the ruler scrubber with bookmarks; the micro progress; the webtoon strip (the default layout) with its seams, bands, zoom, chapter swipe, edge HUDs, warmth and colour layers; the chapter-end credits with the Coming up card and pull to continue; the end-of-series notices; every reader state; the Contents sheet (phones) and left column panel (tablets); auto-hide, cinema mode and lock mode; the hardware-keyboard map; the opt-in volume keys; the screen-reader semantics. Two skin-neutral engine commands land here for both skins: `pinchZoom` and `zoomAt` (glass §15.4 commit order step 2), together with a skin-neutral reader-preferences record that migrates the device-wide legacy keys into per-profile values. The 120 Hz strip itself (feed, extents, decode budget, restore, prefetch) is not touched. Both ways into a chapter, `ReaderScreen` (library manifest) and `SourceReaderScreen` (source reader payload), are proven. When you finish, the ScreenIds `reader` and `readerLanding` leave the Cinematic `PENDING` set. Paged layouts, read-all, the Reading setup sheet, the right Margins panel and page actions are `mobile/13`; guided view, page tint, soundscape and the auto-scroll chip are `mobile/23`.

### A. Skin-neutral engine and data work (`mobile/lib/features/`, no pixels, first commits)

A1. **`pinchZoom(Offset focal, double scale, double velocity, {required double min, required double max, double? snapStep, bool rubberBand = true})` and `zoomAt(Offset point, double scale, {required Duration duration, required Curve curve, SpringDescription? spring})`** on `ReaderEngine` (glass §15.4; cinematic §8.14.5). Today the strip zooms by scaling the page width inside the list and double tap toggles 1.0 ⇄ 2.0 unanchored (mobile.md §6a). The new commands keep the content point under `focal` or `point` fixed while the width scales: the vertical scroll offset is corrected in the same frame (`newOffset = (offset + focal.dy) × newScale / oldScale − focal.dy`), and above 1.0× horizontal overflow pans (reuse the engine's horizontal pan if `mobile/00` kept one; otherwise wrap the strip in a horizontal `SingleChildScrollView` only while zoom > 1.0, never at ≤ 1.0, so the resting strip keeps exactly one scrollable). Past the limits the scale rubber-bands with `d·(1 − 1/(x·0.35/d + 1))` (§4.4, `scalarRubber`) and settles back on release. Cinematic passes `duration: durLine` (240 ms) and `curve: CineCurves.settle`; Glass will pass a spring. Put the pure math in `mobile/lib/features/reader/engine/zoom_math.dart` (`focalOffset(offset, focalY, oldScale, newScale)`, the horizontal equivalent, `clampZoom`, `snapZoom(value, 0.1)`, `rubberBand(x, d, 0.35)`) with `mobile/test/features/reader/engine/zoom_math_test.dart` proving: the focal point stays fixed within 0.5 px for 1 → 2 and 2 → 0.5, 1.26 snaps to 1.3, clamping to 0.5–3.0, the rubber band at x = 0 and x = d.
A2. **Pinch without touching the one-finger path.** The pinch is read by a `Listener` on the strip that counts pointers. When a second pointer lands, the strip's physics switch to `NeverScrollableScrollPhysics` for that gesture and the scale is computed from the two pointers (focal = their midpoint, scale = distance ÷ start distance), feeding `pinchZoom`; when fewer than two pointers remain, it releases with the scale velocity, snaps to 0.1 and restores the physics. No `ScaleGestureRecognizer` joins the arena of a one-finger drag, so single-finger scrolling runs exactly as before (the 120 Hz path). This is a deliberate, recorded deviation from the words "Flutter `ScaleGestureRecognizer` driving the engine's zoom" in cinematic §8.14.5: a scale recognizer in the arena competes with the strip's one-finger vertical drag, while the pointer-counting `Listener` gives the same focal pinch, limits and snap. Keep the `Listener`, do not switch to `ScaleGestureRecognizer`, and list the deviation in the report. Widget test: a two-pointer gesture changes zoom and a one-pointer drag of the same distance scrolls without changing it.
A3. **Double-tap parameters.** The engine's tap classifier takes `doubleTapWindow` and `doubleTapSlop` as parameters with today's legacy values as defaults (280 ms), so the legacy reader is unchanged. The Cinematic skin passes the shared thresholds of §11: tap slop 8 px, double tap ≤ 300 ms and ≤ 24 px apart. Tests for both parameter sets.

### C. The reader route: one `SwipeablePage` (§8.14.2, §8.0.5, §15.3)

C2. `transitionDuration` for `wipe` is (200 + (c − 1) × 16) + 40 + (280 + (c − 1) × 16) ms for c blades: **616 ms** with 4 blades (width < 600) and **744 ms** with 8 blades (width ≥ 600); for `dip` it is **440 ms**; `reverseTransitionDuration` is **440 ms**. Under reduced motion (`CineMotion.reduced(context)` read when the page is built) they are 200 ms (wipe, `durClip`) and 150 ms (dip, `durReduced`).
C3. The `transitionBuilder` (0.4.8 shape `(context, animation, secondaryAnimation, isSwipeGesture, child)`):
   - `animation.status == forward` and entry `wipe`: N `#000000` blades as `Transform(transform: Matrix4.diagonal3Values(1, s, 1), alignment: …)`. Blade edges land on the grid's column edges: 4 columns (margin 16, gutter 12) under 600 px, 8 columns (margin 32, gutter 16) at 600 px and above (§2.2.2); blade i runs from column i's left edge to column i + 1's left edge (the first from x = 0, the last to the right edge), exactly `mobile/06` item 4.7's `wipe_geometry.dart` rects, which this step reuses (§8.14.2 "blade edges land on column edges"). Blade i closes `scaleY 0 → 1` from the top edge over `Interval(i·16 / T, (i·16 + 200) / T, curve: CineCurves.settle)`, the screen holds black 40 ms (`durHoldDip`), then blade i opens `scaleY 1 → 0` toward the bottom edge over `Interval((close + 40 + i·16) / T, (close + 40 + i·16 + 280) / T, curve: CineCurves.settle)`, where close = 200 + (c − 1) × 16 and T is the total. The incoming page is invisible until the hold. Blades animate transform only.

### I. Auto-hide, cinema, lock and focus (§8.14.3, §14.4, §14.5)

I1. I2. While the chrome's `FocusScopeNode` has focus, auto-hide is suspended. When `m`, `c` or a tap on the page hides the chrome while it holds focus, focus moves first to the reading surface.
I3. After a tap opened the chrome, hide after 3000 ms idle (paused while a sheet or the ruler is in use). Never hide during the first 800 ms of a chapter. When a screen reader is running (`MediaQuery.accessibleNavigationOf(context)`), the chrome never auto-hides (§14.5).

I8.
